    Data sources

    Home values and rents: Data Provided by Zillow Group, per-capita income (historical): U.S. BEA via FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, recent-year income estimates (both measures): U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, QCEW, household income: U.S. Census Bureau SAIPE via FRED, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, county boundaries: us-atlas (US Census Bureau TIGER/Line), hosted at: underflow.dev/housing, source code: GitHub.

    Notes & caveats
    • Definitely contains inaccuracies.
    • Per-capita income is a mean (all income incl. investments and transfers, per resident), so very high earners can raise it sharply. Household income is a Census median estimate, less precise for small counties.
    • Price / per-capita income compares a home to one person's income, so it runs higher than the usual household-based ratio.
    • Home values are Zillow estimates of a typical home, not sale prices. Rent is typical asking rent on new listings (~1,400 counties, mostly recent years); existing tenants often pay less.
    • The rent ratio uses all households' income; renters typically earn less, so their actual share is higher.
    • Income is annual, interpolated monthly; years not yet published are estimated from each state's wage growth (BLS QCEW), so counties within a state move together in those years (faded in county charts). Dollars are not inflation-adjusted.
    • Gaps: Connecticut income is estimated after 2021–2023 (switch to planning regions); ~15 Virginia jurisdictions lack per-capita income.
